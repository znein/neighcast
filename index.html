    How Neighcast decides

    It pulls two forecasts - Open-Meteo and the National Weather Service - and uses whichever is colder for the coldest "feels like" (wind chill) temperature between 6 pm and 8 am. It flags nights where they disagree. Then it adds wind and any rain or snow, then uses the standard blanketing chart for your horse's coat - unclipped, partly clipped or fully clipped. Wet coats lose their warmth, so rain or snow can bump the call up a level, a run-in shed takes some of the bite out of the wind, and a box stall takes it all. See the full chart.

    How cold is too cold?

    A dry, thick winter coat is excellent insulation. Horses with one don't start burning extra energy to stay warm until about 18°F (Rutgers, University of Minnesota). That's why the Unclipped setting rarely calls for a blanket above 30°F.

    Wet changes everything. A wet or thin coat loses most of that protection, closer to 59°F (Rutgers), which is why rain or snow moves the call up.

    Most horses in mild climates never grow that coat, so they're blanketed like clipped horses (University of Maine). Shelter, plenty of hay and good body condition all help.

    General guidance from university extension services, not veterinary advice. Your vet knows your horse best.
